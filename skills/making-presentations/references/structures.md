# Deck structures by kind

Pick the kind by what the audience must do when the talk ends, not by the label on the meeting. A
case study for a job interview is a consultancy deck; a request for budget is an internal proposal even
when it looks like a course plan. Slide counts below are for a 10-minute talk (about one main slide a
minute, one minute spare); scale with the time.

Every kind shares: a cover, one message per main slide stated in its title, speaker notes that start
with the slide's start time ("2:30.") and say what to say, and a backup section behind a divider slide
with one slide per question the audience is likely to ask.

## Consultancy: the audience decides

Client recommendation, feasibility or sizing study, interview case study, steering committee.
Answer first; the method earns trust but does not lead.

1. Cover: the question, or the answer as a title.
2. The answer (teaser): the recommendation with its numbers, and what was built to get it.
3. The problem in the client's words: what is being decided, under which constraints.
4. What the data say: the two or three facts that shape the answer.
5. How it was solved: one picture of the approach or model, not a tour of methods.
6. Why it is right: the check, bound, validation or comparison that proves it.
7. What could break it: the key assumptions and how much the answer moves with them.
8. Recommendation and next steps: the decision asked for, owners, what happens after.

Backup: full model, every scenario, data cleaning, method comparison, sensitivity per case.

## Research: the audience judges a finding

Conference or seminar talk, thesis or project defence, internal research review.

1. Cover.
2. The question and why it matters, in one slide.
3. What is known and the gap.
4. The finding, stated plainly before the evidence (short talks: put it on slide 2).
5. Method: data and approach, one or two slides.
6. Results: one result per slide, the figure carrying it; two or three slides.
7. Robustness and limits: what was tested, what does not hold.
8. Contribution and open questions.

Backup: proofs, extra experiments, ablations, related work, parameter tables. A 20-minute slot takes
12 to 15 main slides.

## Marketing and sales: the audience must care and take a next step

Company or capability presentation, pitch to a prospect, webinar, trade-fair deck.

1. Cover: the outcome the client gets, not only the company name.
2. Their problem: the pain in their terms.
3. The cost of the status quo: time, money, risk, with a number where one exists.
4. What we do: the offer in one sentence and one picture.
5. Proof: two or three case studies, each on the same layout (goal, what was done, result with a
   number, one image).
   The marketing case studies are the natural first role in a slide library (`powerpoint.md`).
6. Why us: what is different, briefly.
7. How we work together: engagement steps, timing.
8. Call to action: the next step and a contact.

Case libraries (many projects) belong behind a divider, grouped by type, so the presenter picks the
two or three that match the room. A deck sent to be read alone needs more text than a presented one:
make the read-alone version a second export from the same source, not a separate deck.

## Internal project: the audience approves, unblocks or acts

Proposal to management (budget, a new track, hiring), kickoff, status update, retrospective.

Proposal:

1. Cover.
2. The ask: what is requested, what it costs, by when, on one slide.
3. Why now: the problem or opportunity.
4. The plan: phases, people, what each phase delivers.
5. The cost: people, time, money, tools; one table.
6. Risks and how they are limited.
7. Decision and first steps.

Status update: status in one line (on track, at risk, late) and why; progress against the plan;
decisions needed from this audience; risks and issues; next steps with owners and dates.

Names on plan or role slides are proposals unless the audience has already agreed them; say so.
Costs often go to a separate audience: keep them on their own slides so a version without them is one
deletion away.
